🔩 Motherboard Parts

5 basic mobile PCB components

🟫 Resistor
A small component that limits or controls the flow of electric current in a circuit, protecting sensitive parts from too much power.
On mobile PCB: Controls current to LEDs, sensors & small ICs. Identified by colour bands or SMD codes (e.g. "103").
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala (black) alladhu Blue colour block
• Pin side: Two pin-laum mala (black) iron erugum, side-la vera colour erugum
🧪 How to check a resistor (multimeter):
Safety: Disconnect power and the battery before testing. Safely discharge any capacitors.
• Setup: Set the multimeter to resistance (Ω) mode
• Test: Touch the probes to the resistor's two pins. Other parts on the board can affect the reading; lift one resistor lead if needed for an accurate check
• Result: Compare the reading with the resistor's rated value and tolerance. If it is outside that range, investigate further. OL may mean an open resistor, poor probe contact, or a value above the meter's selected range
• Beep: No beep alone does not mean a resistor is faulty. Continuity mode only beeps below the meter's resistance threshold
🌀 Coil (Inductor)
A coiled wire component that stores energy in a magnetic field and smooths/filters current, especially for power supply lines.
On mobile PCB: Found near charging IC & power management IC. Often a small square/round metal-shielded block.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala (black) colour block
• Pin side: Two pin-laum mala (black) iron erugum, side-laum mala (black) colour erugum
🔵 Condenser (Capacitor)
Stores and releases small amounts of electrical energy, used to smooth voltage and filter out noise from the power line.
On mobile PCB: The most common part — tiny black/tan SMD rectangles all over the board, near battery & IC pins.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala brown colour erugum
🔺 Diode
Allows current to flow in only one direction, protecting the circuit from reverse current and converting AC to DC in some paths.
On mobile PCB: Used in charging circuits & battery protection paths. Small black component with a band marking direction.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Band: Adi-la (bottom side-la) oru kodu (line/band) erugum

⬛ IC (Integrated Circuit)
A chip that packs thousands of tiny circuits together to process signals, manage power, or control specific functions of the phone.
On mobile PCB: Includes CPU, power management IC (PMIC), charging IC, audio IC, Wi-Fi/RF IC — the "brains" of the board.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Multiple pin (pala pin) erugum — board-la pin adhigama irundha adhu IC-thaan

🔌 Short

Board short circuit — types, symptoms & checking

⚡ Why does a short happen?
• 💧 Water damage: Water inside the phone can corrode the board and create a short. Check the board carefully for any water damage or corrosion marks
• 🔥 Over-voltage: Higher-than-normal voltage can burn components and cause a short — even a wrong/incorrect battery (wrong current/voltage) can do this. Slight burn/fire marks on the charging (CC) board or battery connector point to a short
• 🖤 Burnt component: A burnt/charred part on the board can itself be the short — a burnt charging pin, or a condenser (capacitor) burnt from heavy use/overload. Check the area for any charred or blackened parts
🔋 Primary Short
A short circuit on the main battery line (VBAT) — the battery supply touches ground before the power IC. The phone goes fully dead: nothing works at all — no display, no vibration, no charging sign.
Symptoms: Completely dead phone. On a DC power supply, current (ampere) flows immediately when the battery is connected, even without pressing the power button.
🔎 How to check (multimeter):
• Setup: Disconnect the battery and set the multimeter to continuity (buzzer) mode
• Test: Black probe on board ground (GND), red probe on the battery connector +ve pin
• Result: A beep (0 ohm / near 0) can be a clue to a primary short on the VBAT line. Confirm with the model's reference before calling it a fault
• Confirm: Reverse the probes and test again — a beep in both directions strengthens the clue
• Inject: Only after you know the real rating of this phone's battery (VBAT) line, a current-limited supply can be used on that line with the battery disconnected. A part that heats up is a clue to the short, not proof of the faulty part
🔄 Secondary Short
A short circuit on a voltage line after the power IC (PMIC output lines feeding the CPU, memory & other sections). The phone turns on, the model name/logo appears, but it keeps restarting — it switches off and on again and again, and never reaches the home screen.
Symptoms: Restart loop — logo shows, then the phone restarts repeatedly. On a DC supply, current jumps high as soon as the power button is pressed. The PMIC or CPU area can heat up.
🔎 How to check:
• Step 1: First confirm there is no short at the battery connector (rule out a primary short)
• Step 2: Identify the relevant PMIC output rails from the schematic or boardview and check those in diode mode. Do not assume every coil is an output line
• Result: A very low reading or a beep on a line can be a clue to a secondary short. Some low-voltage rails normally read low resistance, so confirm with the model's schematic or a known-good board before calling it a fault
• Inject: Only after you know the real rating of the shorted output line, a current-limited supply at a voltage safe for that line can be used. A part that heats up is a clue to the short, not proof of the faulty part
🔍 Finding the shorted component
Once you know which line is shorted, a current-limited supply on that line can help you find where the short is. A part that heats up is a clue, not proof. Confirm before removing anything.
🧪 Voltage injection method (clue only):
• Know the line: Identify the actual shorted line and its normal voltage from the schematic or boardview. Do not choose the voltage from the words input or output alone
• Limits: Use a voltage and a current limit that are safe for that line and phone model. The settings described for this example were 4V (input) and 1V (output); these are not verified limits for another rail or phone
• Inject: Connect minus to board ground and plus to the shorted line, with the battery, USB and any external power source disconnected
• Look: Rosin smoke, freeze spray or a thermal camera can show a part that warms. A warm part is a clue, not proof, because a powered IC can also warm up
• Next: Confirm with measurements before replacing any part
🔎 Current clue: On a DC supply at the battery connector, high current as soon as the board is connected, before the power button, may suggest a short on the input (main, battery-side) line. Current that is normal until the power button is pressed and then rises sharply may suggest a short on an output (switched-on) line. This is a clue, not proof of the fault. It does not name the exact line or part. Current values differ between phones, so no single number works for every board. Before injecting, identify the actual line and use a voltage and current limit that are safe for that line and phone model.

Power button varaikkum normal-a irundhu, press pannina piragu current romba aerina, output (apram on aagura) line-la short irukkalam nu kaattalam. Idhu oru clue mattum, fault confirm illa. Exact line illa part-a sollaadhu. Phone-ku phone current maarum, so ella board-kum oru number work aagaadhu. Injection pannum munnadi actual line-a kandupidichu, andha line-kum phone model-kum safe-aana voltage, current limit use pannunga.
🔎 Extra diagnostic clue: in the method described here, a reading above 100 mA at 1V prompts further investigation; it is not a universal test that confirms a short. Before continuity checks, disconnect the DC supply, battery and USB, and ensure the board is de-energized. Check the suspected capacitor's pads against ground and interpret the readings using the schematic or a known-good board. A continuity beep cannot distinguish a direct ground connection from a short through other components. The fault may be in that capacitor, another component on the connected line, or the IC. Do not remove a capacitor based on current draw or a beep alone. Voltage injection requires a known rail and verified safe voltage and current limits; 1V is not safe for every rail.

⚠️ Disconnect the battery before injecting into a line. Use a voltage and current limit that are safe for that line and phone model. A wrong voltage can damage the board. Short repair needs soldering experience, so work carefully.
