🔩 Motherboard Parts

5 basic mobile PCB components

🟫 Resistor
A small component that limits or controls the flow of electric current in a circuit, protecting sensitive parts from too much power.
On mobile PCB: Controls current to LEDs, sensors & small ICs. Identified by colour bands or SMD codes (e.g. "103").
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala (black) alladhu Blue colour block
• Pin side: Two pin-laum mala (black) iron erugum, side-la vera colour erugum
🧪 How to check a resistor (multimeter):
Safety: Disconnect power and the battery before testing. Safely discharge any capacitors.
• Setup: Set the multimeter to resistance (Ω) mode
• Test: Touch the probes to the resistor's two pins. Other parts on the board can affect the reading; lift one resistor lead if needed for an accurate check
• Result: Compare the reading with the resistor's rated value and tolerance. If it is outside that range, investigate further. OL may mean an open resistor, poor probe contact, or a value above the meter's selected range
• Beep: No beep alone does not mean a resistor is faulty. Continuity mode only beeps below the meter's resistance threshold
🌀 Coil (Inductor)
A coiled wire component that stores energy in a magnetic field and smooths/filters current, especially for power supply lines.
On mobile PCB: Found near charging IC & power management IC. Often a small square/round metal-shielded block.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala (black) colour block
• Pin side: Two pin-laum mala (black) iron erugum, side-laum mala (black) colour erugum
🔵 Condenser (Capacitor)
Stores and releases small amounts of electrical energy, used to smooth voltage and filter out noise from the power line.
On mobile PCB: The most common part — tiny black/tan SMD rectangles all over the board, near battery & IC pins.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Center: Mala brown colour erugum
🔺 Diode
Allows current to flow in only one direction, protecting the circuit from reverse current and converting AC to DC in some paths.
On mobile PCB: Used in charging circuits & battery protection paths. Small black component with a band marking direction.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Two pin erugum
• Band: Adi-la (bottom side-la) oru kodu (line/band) erugum
⬛ IC (Integrated Circuit)
A chip that packs thousands of tiny circuits together to process signals, manage power, or control specific functions of the phone.
On mobile PCB: Includes CPU, power management IC (PMIC), charging IC, audio IC, Wi-Fi/RF IC — the "brains" of the board.
🔎 Eppadi kandupidippathu:
• Pin: Multiple pin (pala pin) erugum — board-la pin adhigama irundha adhu IC-thaan

🔌 Short

Board short circuit — types, symptoms & checking

⚡ Why does a short happen?
• 💧 Water damage: Water inside the phone can corrode the board and create a short. Check the board carefully for any water damage or corrosion marks
• 🔥 Over-voltage: Higher-than-normal voltage can burn components and cause a short — even a wrong/incorrect battery (wrong current/voltage) can do this. Slight burn/fire marks on the charging (CC) board or battery connector point to a short
• 🖤 Burnt component: A burnt/charred part on the board can itself be the short — a burnt charging pin, or a condenser (capacitor) burnt from heavy use/overload. Check the area for any charred or blackened parts
🔋 Primary Short
A short circuit on the main battery line (VBAT) — the battery supply touches ground before the power IC. The phone goes fully dead: nothing works at all — no display, no vibration, no charging sign.
Symptoms: Completely dead phone. On a DC power supply, current (ampere) flows immediately when the battery is connected, even without pressing the power button.
🔎 How to check (multimeter):
• Setup: Disconnect the battery and set the multimeter to continuity (buzzer) mode
• Test: Black probe on board ground (GND), red probe on the battery connector +ve pin
• Result: A beep (0 ohm / near 0) means there is a primary short on the VBAT line
• Confirm: Reverse the probes and test again — a beep in both directions confirms the short
• Inject: Give 4V on the battery (VBAT) line with the battery disconnected — the shorted component heats up
🔄 Secondary Short
A short circuit on a voltage line after the power IC (PMIC output lines feeding the CPU, memory & other sections). The phone turns on, the model name/logo appears, but it keeps restarting — it switches off and on again and again, and never reaches the home screen.
Symptoms: Restart loop — logo shows, then the phone restarts repeatedly. On a DC supply, current jumps high as soon as the power button is pressed. The PMIC or CPU area can heat up.
🔎 How to check:
• Step 1: First confirm there is no short at the battery connector (rule out a primary short)
• Step 2: Check the coils (inductors) near the PMIC in diode mode — each one is an output line
• Result: If any line reads 0 / beeps, there is a secondary short on that output line
• Inject: Give only 1V on the shorted output line — the shorted component heats up
🔍 Finding the shorted component
Once you know which line is shorted, inject a small voltage into that line and find which component heats up — that heating part is the faulty one.
🧪 Voltage injection method:
• Setup: Set the DC power supply with a current limit (1A). Voltage: 4V for a primary short (battery/VBAT line), 1V for a secondary short (output line)
• Inject: Connect the minus probe to board ground and the plus probe to the shorted line
• Find: Use rosin smoke / freeze spray / a thermal camera — the component that heats up is the faulty one
• Fix: Remove that component and replace it with a new part of the same value
⚠️ Disconnect the battery before every test. Use only the correct voltage — 4V for a primary short, 1V for a secondary short. More voltage can damage the board. Short repair needs soldering experience, so work carefully.
